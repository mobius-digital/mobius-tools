Locus, audited top to bottom

Every rail item, tab, sheet and menu on desktop, iPad and iPhone, in Agency view and as Lucky Golf. What each screen is for, who uses it, what is wrong, and the one system that fixes it. Cole's own words are the yardstick, and every finding below points back to one of them.

The short version. Locus does not have a design problem so much as a no-shared-skeleton problem. Every feature since the v3 shell brought its own page layout, its own injected stylesheet, its own tags and its own paragraph of instructions, so each one lands looking like a different product. The fix is one page anatomy, one component kit and one copy voice, applied page by page, with the objects (ads, tasks, posts, briefs, reports, leaks) linked to each other wherever they appear. Phase 1 (the shared shell, Home and Meta on it) is already built in the branch linked at the end.

What is actually wrong

123 screenshots (desktop 1440, iPad 1024, iPhone 390; Agency view and Lucky Golf; every rail item, every tab, every sheet, menu and modal I could open) and a read of the shell code say the same seven things. They are root causes, and every per-screen finding below is one of these showing up somewhere.

  1. No shared page skeleton. pageHead() gives a title and three quiet buttons; everything under it is each file's own idea. Tasks, Production, the call screens, Social, Social Week, the leak finder, Calendar and the review viewer each inject their own CSS (#cscss, #soccss, #swcss, .tk-*, .pd-*, .lk-*). Four button styles, two tab styles, three chip styles and three ways to say where something came from are live at once.
  2. Every page opens with a lecture. The purpose line under the title, then a sentence of instructions, then a card whose first line is another sentence. Calendar at 1024px shows 180px of prose before the calendar. Social's first screen is three paragraphs and a form. The reader came for the thing, not the manual.
  3. Tags where placement should do the work. "Automatic", "From Slack", "You add", "Optional" on the call screens; "SHOPIFY", "TW", "BLENDED", "PLATFORMS" on tiles; "Check first", "Worth doing" on leaks. A thing Locus filled in looks like text; a thing you type looks like a field. That difference should be the design, not a coloured chip.
  4. Native controls in the newest screens. Owner is a Windows select on Production cards and the call screens, due dates are the browser's date input, "Who" is a free text box. The task sheet already has the right pieces (avatar pill, calendar popover); they were not reused.
  5. The top of every channel page is a wall. The leak card (300 to 600px of prose per page, with an AI check line, a suggestion line, a who line and a push back) sits above the numbers on Meta, Google, Website and Email, and takes the first two screens of Home. It is the most valuable engine in Locus wearing the worst clothes.
  6. Silos. The weekly report names ads but shows none. The Monday call shows six ad thumbnails but Dartee's "already being made" list cannot be clicked. Leaks link to a page, not to the ad set or the task. Social's "Make ours" lands in Studio but nothing on the Calendar or Production says it came from a post. Tasks approved on a call do not show on the report they were promised in.
  7. The phone is the desktop squeezed. Titles stack with a 40px gap, then two orphan buttons (Metrics, Help) float right; tables keep one column plus a scrollbar; the Monday call's bottom bar loses Next and the clock; Settings' left nav collapses into a card with two tiny links; Social Week is an empty hour grid you scroll sideways. Sheets, the Ask tab and the More sheet are the only parts that feel native, and they prove the phone can feel right.

Two things are right and must survive the redesign: the information itself (Day check, Today's calls, Customers, P&L, the leak engine's content) and the v2 tile and chart components on Home, All channels, Store and Reports. Those pages are the best ones because they share components. The audit is mostly "make everything else share them too".

What the teardown of Later, Sandcastles and Virlo found (sibling session, 89 screenshots: the teardown page). Five rules it adds to the whole-app system, not only to Social:

  1. Chrome before content. Breadcrumb, a 32px title, a two-line subtitle, the Tour / Metrics / Help pills, the segment, a warning strip and a boxed filter form put the first card 600px down at 1280 wide; Virlo shows cards 180px from the top. The page name lives in the tabs row at about 20px, one-clause subtitle or none, the helper pills live in the header menu.
  2. Filter bars styled as settings forms. Uppercase labels over 40px selects inside a bordered card. Research tools use one 36px row of value-as-label pill menus (the period menu style) plus one Filters popover. This applies to every Locus list screen.
  3. Card and number hierarchy. Equal-weight numbers in one clipped 12px row, five same-size tiles in the post panel. One big number per object, the comparison as a pill beside it, the rest small and grey. Social cards get the native 9:16 frame with one overlay per corner.
  4. Vocabulary leaks. The Week tray says "Meta ad, $2,647 spent, 1.3x" inside an organic scheduler; topic names print where account handles go; "1388x" shows for a 9-follower account (the outlier ratio needs a floor: 8+ posts and a 1k+ median). Each screen uses only its own reader's words.
  5. Phone. Three screens of chrome before a card, and an hour grid with two empty days. Bottom tab bar, filters in a bottom sheet, content by the second screen; time grids become day lists; viewers and composers are full-height bottom sheets.

Also for the system: warnings such as low credits belong in settings and the 5am card, never in the content column; an empty state is one sentence and one button; status is one line ("Pulling Takomo and 3 more, last pull 2h ago") instead of a strip.

Twenty patterns they share

  1. One left sidebar is the navigation; horizontal tabs live only inside a page.
  2. The sidebar is grouped by job, at most three levels deep.
  3. Personal items (Inbox, My tasks) sit apart, usually on top.
  4. Search or Cmd+K sits at the top with its key shown.
  5. The sidebar collapses to an icon rail; the rail is what iPads get.
  6. Page header anatomy is fixed: title + actions, tabs, one control row.
  7. Date range and compare are one pill, not a form.
  8. Saved views beat filter rows.
  9. KPI tile = small label, big number, one delta chip, sparkline.
  10. Colour is for status and one accent, never decoration; gradients only on chart strokes.
  11. Dark = neutral graphite + alpha-white hairlines; elevation is a lighter surface, not a shadow.
  12. Tables for money and records; cards for creative and media.
  13. Row detail opens as a side peek; modals are for create, confirm, destroy.
  14. On phones, detail and pickers come up from the bottom.
  15. A 4 to 5 item bottom tab bar plus a floating create button is the phone shell.
  16. One sans at 13 / 14 / 16, semibold not bold, tabular numbers.
  17. Radius 6 controls, 8 to 12 cards and menus, pills for chips.
  18. Empty states say what the thing is and give one action, inline.
  19. Loading is skeletons in the final layout, never a spinner page.
  20. Motion is short, eased out, only on state changes; keyboard actions never animate.

The system

One navigation model for three devices, one page anatomy, one type scale, one colour rule, one kit, one motion rule. This is what phase 1 ships as profit/lx.css (tokens and components) and profit/lx.js (shell, side panel, sheet, menu, palette). Everything below is a rule a future feature can follow without asking.

Navigation: one model, three widths

Desktop, 1180px and up

240px rail: client picker (Agency view on top), Find (Cmd+K), then the destinations exactly as today, then Tools, Settings and you in the foot. The rail never scrolls: it collapses to icons on windows under 760px tall. No right sidebar; the Strategist is a panel.

iPad, 760 to 1180px

The same rail as a 56px icon rail with labels on hover and long press; one tap on the logo expands it over the page. The page keeps its desktop anatomy at 1024 (tiles in 3s, side panel 400px over the content). No hamburger drawer.

iPhone, under 760px

Bottom bar: Home · Ads · Ask · Work (or Store for a client) · More. The page header is one 44px row (title, one action, "..."); tabs are a scrolling pill row; everything else is a bottom sheet. Detail = full-height sheet, not a push.

Page anatomy, every page, no exceptions

  1. Header row (52px): breadcrumb above a 22px title with the serif accent word; the page's actions on the right (one primary button, one quiet button, a "..." menu). Tour, Metrics and Help live inside "...". Nothing from the page ever goes into the top bar.
  2. Tabs row (40px) when the page has views: underline tabs. A platform's jobs (Overview, Campaigns, Ads, Changes) are a segmented control in the control bar, not a second tab row.
  3. Control bar (40px): one row of pills: period + compare, attribution, view, filters, brand (in Agency view). On phones it scrolls sideways in one line.
  4. Strip (optional, 44px): one line that needs attention (the biggest leak, the next calendar date, a report waiting). One line, a number, a link. It folds; it never stacks.
  5. Content: the question's answer first (hero or tile row), then the chart, then the table or list, then secondary cards. Each section has a 15px title and a caption, nothing longer. Explanations live in the Metrics sheet.
  6. Side panel (420px, from the right; full-height sheet on a phone) for any ad, task, post, brief, leak, order or customer. Modals only for create, confirm, destroy.

Spacing, radius, colour

Spacing

4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48. Control height 32 (40 on touch). Table rows 40 (36 dense). Card padding 16. Section gap 24. Page gutter 24 desktop, 16 phone. Rail item 32 tall.

Radius

4 inline tags, 6 buttons and inputs and tabs, 8 cards and menus, 12 panels and modals, 16 bottom sheets (top corners), pill for chips and avatars. The 10px card and the 99px rectangle button go.

Colour

The graphite scale stays as is. One accent (Locus blue) for the primary button, active tab, active rail icon, links, focus and the current series. Good / warn / bad only with a sign or a word, as text and small chips, never as a card border or background unless the whole card is the warning. Channel palette fixed. Gradients only on chart fills and the Ask face. Light theme from the same tokens.

Motion

  • 120ms hover and press, 160ms menus, 200ms side panel and tab underline, 240ms sheets and modals. Ease out on enter, ease in on exit.
  • Only transform and opacity move. Height never animates. Charts re-draw by sliding the series, not by going blank.
  • Keyboard actions, sorts and ticks on a task do not animate; the tick fills and the row leaves after 400ms.
  • Phone sheets use a short spring with no overshoot; swipe to dismiss follows the finger.
  • prefers-reduced-motion swaps everything for an 80ms fade.

The flows that close the silos

  1. Call → report → brief. When Finish on the Monday call approves "New ads to make" and "For the client", those lines appear on the weekly report under This week (already written), on the Daily Brief's "What we're doing" line (new), and the batch rows appear on Production with a "from Monday call Oct 20" link back (new). Ticking a task done shows on the next report's "Done this week" (new).
  2. Report → ad. Every ad named in a report (winners, losers, the Meta section) is a media card that opens the ad panel, on the page and in the client's copy. No "see every ad in Locus" hop.
  3. Leak → task or call. Every leak has "Make it a task" (owner from the leak's who, due Friday) and, for Meta and Google leaks, "Open the call" which opens the ad set in Today's calls. A leak that became a task shows the task's state on the Leaks screen.
  4. Post → brief → calendar. "Make ours" from a post creates a Production card with the post attached (the thumbnail, the beats, the link), and the card's launch date puts it on the Calendar as a Post. The Social Week shows Production's Ready cards in its tray with their brief, not raw Meta ads.
  5. Everything → Find. Cmd+K finds an ad by name, a task by its title, a post by its handle, a brief by its number, and opens the panel from any page.

Where leaks live

Today the leak finder is a card at the top of Meta, Google, Search terms, TikTok, Website and Email (that page's top three, 300 to 600px each) and a six-row card on Home. The content is the best thing Locus writes. The placement is wrong because a leak is a to-do with a price, and a to-do list does not belong above the dashboard it came from.

Decision. Three places, one engine, nothing else:

  1. One strip per page (44px, under the control bar): "Meta is leaking about $3.4K a month in 2 places · Black Wedges | Still is on the cut line · See both". It folds to the number when there is more than one; it is absent when there is none. On Home the strip carries the biggest leak and the total.
  2. Home › Leaks, a page tab beside Overview and Day check: the full ranked list as list rows (money, title, area chip, who, state: open / task made / pushed back), grouped by area, with the AI check button and the lessons at the bottom. This is where the push-back conversation lives.
  3. The leak panel (side panel from the strip, the list, Today's calls or the morning card): the measured number, the benchmark and its source, the cross-channel read, the suggestion, Apply where Locus can act, Make it a task, Open the call, Push back. Everything the card says today, in a panel you open on purpose.

What goes: the per-page top-3 cards, the AI-check paragraph on every card, the "Every leak for Lucky Golf" footer link, the "Where X is leaking" headings. The Strategist's leaks view and the morning card line stay as they are.

Copy voice

Rules, then examples from the screens. The voice is a strategist talking to a colleague: short, specific, in Cole's vocabulary (CPA, ROAS, spend, ad set), never explaining what Locus is doing on its own behalf.

  1. Labels, not sentences. A section title is two to four words. The caption under it is at most one line and says where the thing goes or came from, not how to use it.
  2. Placement says the source. What Locus filled in reads as content. What you type is a field with a placeholder. No "Automatic / From Slack / You add" tags.
  3. Instructions live in the control. The placeholder says what to type ("Ahsan pause the retargeting ad set Friday"), the button says what happens ("Make 2 briefs"). Not a paragraph above the box.
  4. Never narrate the system. Cut "Locus works out who and when", "nothing is written before that", "Locus reads the numbers and answers in plain words", "numbers refresh hourly". The product does its job quietly; Metrics explains it when asked.
  5. One comparison per number. "$10,039, down 14% on last week." Not forecast and prior week and plan.
  6. Say the thing, then the source once. "Cart to checkout on phones is 28%; 45% is normal (Littledata, Oct 2026)." Not a "Measured / Good looks like / Suggestion / who" ladder.
  7. No chip under a chip. One status per card, one per row. The rest is in the panel.
  8. Empty states are one line with one verb. "No requests yet. Send something for approval." Not a dashed box.
  9. The brand's name once per screen, in the title. Not in every caption ("for Lucky Golf", "Lucky Golf's", "across Lucky Golf").
