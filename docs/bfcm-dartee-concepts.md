## Batch: October giveaway, "Game of Belts", 8 ads (NOT RUNNING, build first)

Offer (Cole, Oct 14): win 50 Dartee belts, 25 for you and 25 for your buddy, $4,500 total. Enter free with email
and phone. Not a foursome. Templates (docs/bfcm-ad-templates.md): 1 offer-first (the prize is the offer), 3 sign-up,
7 native phone.

Photos: Justin's Drive folder (1aPLUgdRD3VYFjbbnoRY98lAQLoY40zFV), now a Dartee library source in Locus, so Studio's
"From the library" shows it. Their 15 finished concepts (the throne, "One stack for you. One for your partner.",
"Your partner owes you.") are the direction; we build ours on the RAW shots:
- LEAN: DSC00254-2.jpg, guy leaning on a box stack against a white sweep (light stand and floor visible).
- BELTS: DSC00144-2.jpg, close-up of a pile of textured belts (camo, ostrich, croc, pink, green).
- LABELS: IMG_9995.JPG, tight stack with the box labels readable (Albino Gator).
- Video stills from the .MOV files (two tall stacks; a guy holding a stack taller than him; a guy breaking through
  the paper sweep with the stack).

Every raw gets the same edit in Studio ("Use as the ad", "what to change on the photo"): clean bright white seamless,
remove the light stands, cables and floor edge, lift the colours so the box greens and pinks pop, crisp contrast. Never
the flat raw look. Two lines stack photos on top of each other (Cole: "stack them"), like their examples.

1. **Angle:** One stack for you, one for your buddy. **Why:** the prize is the offer and two real stacks show it in
   one look; their own concept, rebuilt on the real photo. **Concept:** the two-stack still, cleaned and brightened,
   headline between the stacks: "25 for you. 25 for your buddy." Small: "Win 50 Dartee belts. Enter free."
   **Testing:** the control. Template 1.
2. **Angle:** A guy and his prize. **Why:** a person makes the pile real and gives it scale. **Concept:** LEAN photo,
   stands and floor removed, warm bright grade. Headline down the left side: "Win 50 belts." Small: "You and your
   buddy. $4,500 in belts." **Testing:** a person with the prize vs stacks alone (line 1). Template 1.
3. **Angle:** It's the belts, not the boxes. **Why:** colour and texture sell Dartee; boxes don't show what you win.
   **Concept:** BELTS close-up, rich grade, "25 of these are yours." Small: "25 more for your buddy. Enter free."
   **Testing:** the belts vs the boxes. Template 1.
4. **Angle:** Stacked (two photos, one ad). **Why:** Cole's stacking idea: the box pile on top, the belts that are
   inside them below, so the eye goes from "that's a lot" to "that's what I get". **Concept:** top half the LABELS
   stack, bottom half the BELTS close-up, one line across the seam: "50 belts. Half are yours." **Testing:** a two-photo
   stack vs a single photo. Template 1.
5. **Angle:** Enter for your buddy. **Why:** the buddy is the share mechanic; entering for him is a favour he will
   hear about. Their best line was "Your partner owes you." **Concept:** the still of the guy holding a stack taller
   than he is, graded, "Enter for your buddy. He'll owe you forever." **Testing:** the favour angle. Template 1.
6. **Angle:** The group text. **Why:** a buddy giveaway lives in the text thread; native phone is untested for us and
   cheap to try. **Concept:** iMessage thread over a soft, blurred stack: "entered us both in the dartee thing" /
   "what thing" / "50 belts. 25 each" / "ok you're my favorite playing partner now". **Testing:** native chat vs a
   photo ad. Template 7.
7. **Angle:** Takes 20 seconds. **Why:** the only cost is the form; showing it is short removes the friction, and the
   page's own line asks for the phone number. **Concept:** a phone showing the entry form (email, phone) leaning on
   the LABELS stack: "Where should we text you if you win?" **Testing:** showing the form vs selling the prize.
   Template 3.
8. **Angle:** What $4,500 looks like. **Why:** the number does the work only next to the pile. **Concept:** the
   still of the guy bursting through the paper sweep with the stack (stacked over the two-stack shot for height),
   "This is $4,500 in belts. Yours and your buddy's." **Testing:** the dollar number vs the belt count (line 1).
   Template 1. From Nov 9, the same ad with "Entries close Nov 16".

Copy: "buddy" in our lines (Dartee's own ads say "playing partner"; either is fine). No em dashes. No "foursome".
Still needed to launch: the signup page link.

## Batch: Early Bird, 15 ads (live Nov 1 to 16, done by Oct 18)

Offer (Season, locked Oct 6): buy one belt, get a second 50% off, any belt. Lighter than the weekend on purpose.
Built on 50% (50 vs 60 still open; only the number changes). The season opens on gift shopping, so half the lines
talk to the golfer buying two for himself and his group, half to the person buying for a golfer.

Product images: real Dartee photography from the Locus library (64 photos) and Shopify, ticked as studio shots,
with the product fingerprint on. Where a line names a past ad's image, use that photo as the ad ("Use as the ad")
and let Studio add only the words. Proof is our own Meta ad ids.

Suggested ad sets (judged ad set first): A Offer (1, 2, 5, 6, 13, 15), B Gift (3, 9, 10, 11), C The belt (4, 7, 8,
12, 14).

1. **Angle:** The offer, plainly. **Why:** the plain two-belt BOGO graphic was the best static of the warehouse sale
   (357-13, 9.5x on Meta). **Concept:** two belts (one textured leather, one Raincheck) crossing on a dark field, big
   type: "Buy a belt. Get the second 50% off." Small: "Any belt. Nov 1 to 16." **Testing:** the control every other
   line is read against. Format: Offer-first banner. Ref: 357-13 (120246489082880741); category: Anson two-offer split m1240775794544276.
2. **Angle:** One for you, one for your buddy. **Why:** "One for you. One for your homie." was the best video of the
   warehouse sale (359-2, 3.8x on Meta); the buddy is who the second belt is for. **Concept:** two belts side by side
   on a cart seat, a hand-written tag on each: "Me" and "Mike". Headline: "One for you. Half off for your buddy."
   **Testing:** the buddy framing against the plain offer (1). Format: Sticky notes on product. Ref: 359-2
   (120246488431680741); category: Sunday Golf handwritten post-it on a course photo, early BF from Nov 2,
   21 days (m1909897476240753).
3. **Angle:** His belt and his dad's, one order. **Why:** gift shopping starts Nov 1; two golfers on one list is the
   most common gift-buyer problem, and the second belt at half solves it. **Concept:** phone Notes app, "Christmas
   list": "Jake: golf belt ✓" / "Dad: golf belt ✓ (half off)" over a photo of two boxed belts. **Testing:** gift-list
   framing for the person buying, not the golfer. Format: Notes app. Category: "Gift one for yourself / gift one to
   someone you love" ran 275 days on this exact mechanic (m1634344711059710).
4. **Angle:** One for the course, one for dinner. **Why:** the buckle comes off and swaps, and a textured leather and
   a Raincheck cover both; the self-buyer wants two looks, not two of one. **Concept:** split frame, left a Raincheck
   on a golf polo, right a dark textured leather with jeans; "Saturday morning. Saturday night." Sub: second belt 50%
   off. **Testing:** two-looks reason vs the buddy reason (2). Ref: 257 B (120233266389580741, 5.59x TW, three belts
   on black).
5. **Angle:** The math. **Why:** "$300 for a golf belt?" (2503-7) was the best CPA in September: saying the price
   works when the answer is good. **Concept:** receipt layout: "Textured leather belt $99.99 / Second belt (50% off)
   $49.99 / Total for two $149.98". Headline: "Two belts. Do the math." **Testing:** price shown vs hidden.
   Format: Value stack. Category: Bad Birdie "Save 50%. Do the Math." (m712917008048011).
6. **Angle:** Smart golfers shop early. **Why:** last year's best early static (Early BF C, 2.92x Meta, the most
   efficient early ad). Same layout, new offer, so we learn if the layout or last year's offer carried it.
   **Concept:** phone-frame card, belts across the middle, "Smart golfers shop early. Second belt 50% off."
   **Testing:** last year's winning layout with this year's offer. Ref: Early BF C (Meta ad 120232805385170741);
   category: Bogey Bros "PRE BLACK FRIDAY" kicker over three products with zoom circles, 15 days (m1746937035925437).
7. **Angle:** The belt people notice, now two. **Why:** 353-1 Midnight Black Caiman is the best ROAS static in the
   account (5.39x TW, $21 CPA) and never got budget. **Concept:** that exact photo as the ad, name as the headline,
   a small round sticker: "2nd belt 50% off". **Testing:** a proven product image plus an offer sticker (the sticker
   version). Ref: 353-1 (120246116810010741).
8. **Angle:** Five stars, then the offer. **Why:** the review card is the second-biggest static ad set (298 A, 2.04x
   TW, $50 CPA). **Concept:** the same card layout ("Best Belt Ever", the customer's own words, shortened) with a
   strip under the belt: "Second belt 50% off, Nov 1 to 16". **Testing:** social proof carrying the offer.
   Ref: 298 A (120239960847110741).
9. **Angle:** Customers already buy the second one. **Why:** a real review says "I've already purchased a second
   belt in the last month", and the tweet card format ran at 2.66x TW (261-3). **Concept:** tweet card with that line
   (real reviewer first name, no handle invented) over a white textured belt and a gold buckle. Headline: "The second
   one's half off now." **Testing:** a customer saying the offer's logic. Format: Tweet. Ref: 261-3
   (120233922886840741).
10. **Angle:** The golfer who has everything. **Why:** gift buyers' line: "There is nothing golf related he needs nor
    wants." A belt is the one thing he uses every round and never upgrades. **Concept:** open Dartee box on a kitchen
    counter, ribbon, the whole kit visible. "He has every club. He doesn't have this." Sub: and the second one's half
    off. **Testing:** the gift-buyer objection as the hook. Ref: 250 A gift static (120232222230200741, 3.23x Meta);
    category: Buckley belts in an open magnetic gift box beside a waist shot, 401 days (m1342805034124596).
11. **Angle:** No waist size needed. **Why:** the gift buyer's fear is the wrong size ("bought me a glove but thought I
    would wear a large"); the belt is cut once to fit and adjusts every 1/8 inch. **Concept:** belt with the sizing
    card and scissors, three small labels (cut once, fits up to 46 in, clicks every 1/8 in). "Don't know his size?
    Doesn't matter." **Testing:** removing the sizing objection. Ref: Dec 2025 264-1 labelled-belt layout
    (Meta ad 120234356273280741, 4.76x Meta); category: SlideBelts "the belt GUARANTEED to fit" gift static, 362 days
    (m1545599892992790), Anson "Make Gifting Easy with our Trim-to-Fit Belts" (m914808557287466).
12. **Angle:** Retire both old belts. **Why:** killing the old belt is a proven villain (292 A "Ditch the dad belt",
    299 the toilet drop 2.36x TW ad set). **Concept:** two cracked old belts with stretched holes in a bin, two Dartees
    on the counter beside it. "Two old belts. Two new ones. Second one half off." **Testing:** the villain angle with
    the offer. Ref: 299 B (120240009817870741).
13. **Angle:** Early Bird ends Sunday. **Why:** the back half of a 16-day offer needs a reason to act today.
    **Concept:** a calendar card, Nov 16 circled, two belts. "Second belt 50% off ends Nov 16." **Testing:** deadline
    urgency (load Nov 10). Format: Countdown.
14. **Angle:** Colours go. **Why:** real scarcity, not a made-up number: Bird Hunter, all three braided Dock Line and
    two ostrich colours sold out this fall; last year's "HURRY, BEFORE WE SELL OUT" collage took the most BF spend.
    **Concept:** four-photo collage of textured leathers, "Get the colour before it goes. Second one half off."
    **Testing:** scarcity vs value. Ref: BFCM #15 (Meta ad 120233583014530741).
15. **Angle:** Is Dartee doing Black Friday? **Why:** people wait for Black Friday; meeting the search they would type
    tells them early is worth it. **Concept:** search bar typing "is dartee doing black friday", the answer card
    under it: "Early Bird is on now: second belt 50% off, Nov 1 to 16." **Testing:** native search format.
    Format: Google search. Category: Buckley's search-bar headline static, 401 days (m1342805034124596).

Copy notes for the batch: never "BOGO"; don't name a free-shipping amount; "textured leather", never real gator;
350-1's body copy says "15,000+ golfers": don't carry that line into these ads (no counts).

## The split: AI vs templates vs real photos (Oct 14, proposed, waiting on Cole)

Cole's question: where do the ads come from, and in what share? Dartee statics, last 90 days, Triple Whale
lastPlatformClick, sorted by eye into three kinds:

| Kind | Spend | ROAS | Best of them |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clean belt hero + one short line (studio-style product shot, often rendered) | $8.8K | 2.19 | 257 B 6.05x, 312 B 5.64x, 353-1 5.39x, 350-1 2.01x on $4.3K |
| Designed template (review card, tweet card, text-heavy) | $4.9K | 1.86 | 261-3 tweet 2.55x, 298 A review 1.99x on $2K |
| AI-made scene (new since Sep 18: Bone Dry, Muni, marker) | $1.3K | 1.64 | 365-16 2.79x, 376-3 Bone Dry 2.11x |

Read: the AI scenes are too new and too thin to judge (365 alone had 47 ads), not worse. Bone Dry is the second-best
static of the last two weeks (2.11x, behind the review card 2.27x). The library is old: 41 of its 64 photos are from 2024.

Proposed rule for every batch (20 ads = 8 / 8 / 4):
- **40% AI-made scenes** (Studio draws the world, product fingerprint + score on, one colourway per ad, Bone Dry style).
- **40% templates on proven winners** (the 12 shared layouts; offer-first for promo batches, review and tweet cards).
- **20% real photos, only from NEW shoots** (Justin and James, creators). No more 2024 library shots.
- Each kind in its own ad set so it is judged ad set first; after two weeks move the split toward the winner.
- Promo batches lean templates (offer-first won BFCM 2025): 30 / 50 / 20.
