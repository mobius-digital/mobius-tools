Mobius creative team · one-page guide

Every problem has three layers. Most of our ads only say the first one.

From Donald Miller's Building a StoryBrand: brands sell the fix to the external problem; people buy the fix to the internal one. Every brand in a category says layer one, so a layer-one ad sounds like everyone else's. Use this for ads, creator scripts, briefs, emails, pages and client calls.

The villain

start here
What is causing this?
One root cause. A thing, a habit or an old way of doing it. Never a person, never a named competitor. One villain per ad; a brand can have several, each one its own angle.

1 · External

what they search
What is the physical problem?
What they would type into Google. Needed so people know what it's for. Never enough on its own.

2 · Internal

what they pay for
How does it make them feel?
Embarrassed, flustered, dreading it, feeling old. Nobody searches for it; they lie awake with it. Take it from the customer quotes and tie it to one moment.

3 · Philosophical

why they stay
Why is it wrong that they have to deal with this?
"You shouldn't have to..." It gives the customer a side to take. Only use it when the product can back it up, or it sounds preachy.

RuleChanging the layer changes the reason to buy, so it is a new angle. Test it as an angle, never as a headline.

The layer check (any brief, ad or email)

  1. Which layer does it argue? Write it on the brief's WHY line.
  2. Is that the right layer for who sees it? Cold audience plus layer 1 only is our most common miss.
  3. Is the feeling in the customer's own words, at one real moment? "Feel confident" is nobody's words.
  4. One villain, and it's a thing, not a person or a competitor?
  5. If there's a "should" line, can this product back it up?
