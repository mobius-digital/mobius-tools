## 1. The science in plain words

**The first second decides whether there is a second second.** On TikTok the feed makes its first keep-or-skip read at about 1.5 seconds and the distribution decision by 3 seconds, from OpusClip's scoring of 34,635 viral TikToks (https://www.opus.pro/blog/viral-tiktoks-first-3-seconds-2026). TikTok's own creative guidance says "Introduce your content proposition in the first 3 seconds" and "Prioritize your hook in the first 6 seconds" (https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/creative-best-practices).

**Even people who leave early still count, which is why the first frame matters more than the script.** Nielsen research commissioned by Meta found that people who watched a video ad for under three seconds still showed a 47% lift in ad recall, 32% in brand awareness and 44% in purchase intent (https://marketingdive.com/news/brand-lift-happens-in-less-than-1-second-of-video-study-finds/377333). Most of the recall and awareness value of a TikTok ad lands inside the first six seconds, per TikTok's internal analysis reported by Marketing Brew (https://marketingbrew.com/stories/2023/12/18/pay-attention-to-me-report-shares-brand-tips-for-catching-users-interest-on-tiktok).

**What the platforms and the ad tools actually measure.**
- Hook rate (also called thumbstop): 3-second video plays divided by impressions. Motion's glossary calls 20 to 40% "generally solid" (https://motionapp.com/library/glossary/thumbstop-rate-hook-rate). Jetfuel's 2026 DTC benchmarks: strong is about 30%+, in-feed prospecting sits in the low-to-mid 20s, Reels runs near 30% (https://jetfuel.agency/creative-performance-metrics-for-dtc-video-ads-hold-rate-thumbstop-and-thruplay-benchmarks-2026/). Taylor Sicard's 2026 benchmarks: under 25% is the fix-it zone, 30 to 40% good, 40%+ elite (https://taylorsicard.com/blog/meta-ads-creative-benchmarks-2026).
- Hold rate: of the people who stayed 3 seconds, how many are still there at about 15 seconds (15s views divided by 3s views). Jetfuel: DTC feed prospecting 12 to 25%, median near 18%, Reels near 23%. Sicard: under 15% fix-it, about 25% good, 35%+ elite.
- The most common failure in DTC video is a high hook rate with a low hold rate. Jetfuel's diagnosis: "The opening promises something the ad never delivers, so people bail the second they realize it." Sicard calls it the opening writing "a check the rest of the ad cannot cash." Fix the body, not the hook.
- ThruPlay: a play to completion if the video is under 15 seconds, or at least 15 seconds if longer. Motion's Evan Lee warns that a thru-play fires at 15 seconds, so cross-check with average watch time (https://motionapp.com/library/talk/creative-strategy-explained-the-3-jobs-of-a-hook-bootcamp-week-1-evan-lee-motion/).

**Why 3 to 8 seconds is its own problem.** OpusClip's retention data puts the secondary drop at 8 to 10 seconds ("hook creates curiosity but follow-up fails to deliver"), then natural decay points at 15 and 30 seconds; videos with a visual change roughly every 4 seconds averaged 58% retention versus 41% for static talking-head videos, and captions added about 12% (https://www.opus.pro/blog/tiktok-length-format-retention-data). Influencers Time says to stop thinking of the hook as seconds 0 to 3 and treat it as "a rhythm that repeats every 5-8 seconds," with the second micro-hook at 8 to 15 seconds because that is "where drop-off typically spikes, so treat it as a second opener" (https://www.influencers-time.com/tiktok-watch-time-feed-rebuild-sponsored-hooks-and-pacing/).

**What a hook has to do.** Motion's Evan Lee: a hook (1) "generates an emotional reaction by cutting through all of the noise," (2) "targets and disqualifies by calling out who it's going after," and (3) "connects naturally to the story"; he puts about 80% of a direct-response ad's effectiveness on the hook (Motion talk above). Jenny Hoyos (1.6B Shorts views): hook "no more than 3 seconds," then foreshadow "what's coming, what the conflict is, what they can expect by the end," use "but/then" turns throughout, and make the ending the most intense beat because the viewer's impression "depends on the intensity of the emotion at the end" (https://marketing-examined.beehiiv.com/p/jenny-hoyos-shortform-video-playbook).

**Why open loops hold people.** The curiosity gap (Loewenstein's information-gap idea) and the Zeigarnik effect (unfinished things stick in the mind) are the two mechanisms every "promise early, pay off late" structure leans on (https://virlo.ai/blog/the-science-behind-what-happens-next, https://blog.autonolab.com/blog/2025-08-22-open-loops-and-curiosity-gaps-the-psychology-of-binge-watching/). The trap is a fake gap: Virlo's piece notes real curiosity gaps "promise value and deliver it," clickbait "fails to satisfy."

**The talking-head nuance (read this before briefing creators).** Two findings look contradictory and are both true:
- A person on screen in the first two seconds helps the hook. VidMob's study of 1,678 TikTok ads and 7.3B impressions (Jan to Oct 2023): a creator or person in the opening moments raised hooking power 50% and ad recognition 32%; direct-to-camera talking head gave a 14% lift in 2-second view-through; everyday people hooked 1.7x more than celebrity talent; a product shot alone lifted 6-second view-through 17%; outdoor locations cut 6-second view-through 26%; a logo up front with no story cut it 14% (https://vidmob.com/resource/tiktok-hook-analysis).
- A person only talking for 30 seconds loses the hold. Opascope's managed book ($30M+ a month) found "storytelling visual hooks outperformed talking-head UGC 86 to 14 percent," where talking-head means "voice-driven, no narrative arc, no b-roll" and a storytelling hook "opens with a story beat instead of a sales beat" (https://opascope.com/insights/ugc-ads/).
So: face in frame early, yes. Face that does nothing for 30 seconds, no. The face should be mid-action.

## 4. Rehooks (mid-video devices that keep people past second 3)

Each: what happens, when, why it holds, where I saw it.

1. **Pivot to a second story at 5s.** Hismile m1406603948018630 opens on the pen-in-the-tube gag, then at 5.7s: "Today we're packing an order for someone who obviously hates us." A new question (who, why) before the first one is even answered. Then at 8.6s a third: "we said we'd never run a free gift promotion ever again." Three loops open inside 9 seconds.
2. **"The 2.0 part matters because..."** Myoovi at 4s names the product and immediately explains why the version number matters (heat on top of TENS). The product name becomes a question, not a label.
3. **Silent test gap, then the reaction.** Drdent m1563635778876692: spoken line ends at 9.9s, nothing until 28.3s, then "F***ing hell. Look at that." The viewer watches a real test because the creator stopped talking.
4. **The second test ("I bet it wears off").** Same ad at 38s: "I'm going to give it a rinse because I reckon once I have a drink, they're going to go back to normal," then at 52s "Wow. Literally nothing's changed." The creator raises her own objection and runs the test again.
5. **Alternating escalation between two people.** Waterboy m1616399766694709: one line each, same number, five rounds, then at 19s the turn: "Wait, it's just one drink." The repetition is the loop; the "wait" is the payoff.
6. **The price flip.** Same Waterboy ad at 22s: "That must have cost a fortune." "I paid $10 for all these things. It's only $2 a stick."
7. **"Watch how this blends."** Jones Road m1564454271790169 at 7s: a hands-on moment before any claim. UGC Roster's body outline puts demonstration second, "prioritizing hands-on footage over talking head."
8. **Self-identification drop at 15s.** Jones Road at 15.4s: "As a 40-year-old, I'm always looking for..." The viewer who matches leans in; the one who does not was never the buyer. Lee's "targets and disqualifies."
9. **The gripe arrives late.** Jones Road at 37.7s: "I was getting so tired of my foundation making me look older." The pain point is a mid-video rehook, not the opener.
10. **"That's what he paid for. This is what he didn't."** Hismile at 37s. A reveal promised at 13s ("you'll see how we brought back...") and paid at 37s.
11. **Situations list that moves the product through the day.** Myoovi at 20 to 26s: "through a work presentation, on the school run, even asleep in bed, nobody will ever notice." Each situation is a new picture.
12. **Letter read-aloud after a skit.** Cherish These m1446107257546440: skit lines until 15.7s ("Take a look at that when you get a sec"), 19s "What is it?", then the engraved message read from 20s to 64s. The reveal question at 19s is the hinge.
13. **"I've done side by side with a cast iron."** HexClad m1404809335121556 at 23s: a comparison enters the middle of a testimonial. Magic Spoon's courtroom scoreboard (15g vs 4g, 1g vs 12g) is the same device as a whole ad (https://thedrsignal.beehiiv.com/p/dr-signal-breakdown-magic-spoon-s-nostalgia-driven-growth-engine, from the search summary).
14. **Prop that comes out at the midpoint.** Ridge m959241849840107 at 13.9s: "If you want more security, get the tracker card. Pairs with your iPhone... slides in like a credit card." A second product as the second hook.
15. **Mid-video CTA or objection handler.** Billo's CTA study: "Six of the top 10 ads included a second CTA earlier in the video before the closing ask," and the #1 ad used a mid-video objection handler ("If you hesitated on any of those... you need [product]") (https://billo.app/creative-insider/inspiration-ideas/cta-examples/).
16. **Change something every 3 to 5 seconds.** Angle switch, B-roll, text change, zoom. Edicion Video Pro's table: angle switch every 5 to 8s, pop-up text 1 to 2s on screen, speed ramps; they claim 40 to 60% higher retention in their own 200-video sample. Socialync: "Keep monologues under 8 seconds before visual changes" (https://www.socialync.io/blog/short-form-video-structure-guide-2026). OpusClip: pattern interrupts every 4s, 58% vs 41%.
17. **Text that updates (a count, a day number, a running tally).** "Day 1 vs Day [X]" (Dan's UGC); Waterboy's on-screen labels change with each round. Socialync: "Number points for viewer progress tracking."
18. **But / then turns.** Jenny Hoyos: "But is that enough to beat Chick-fil-A?" style turns every beat. Hypenest's version: "mini-hooks at each transition point" (https://hypenest.ai/blogs/tiktok-algorithm-2026-video-hooks-retention).
19. **Tease, then reveal, in that order.** OurOwnBrand: show the process before the result, and "drop your stay hook early" by second five (https://ourownbrand.co/10-retention-hacks-to-blow-up-your-tiktok-reels-shorts-in-2025/).
20. **Cut before the natural ending of a beat.** OpusClip rule 7: interrupt mid-sentence or mid-action so the viewer stays for the resolution.
21. **Product enters late on purpose.** Influencers Time on sponsored videos: "Brand and product enter here, not at the top," so the product reads as the answer, not the ad. Cherish These (15s) and Hismile (the offer at 13s) both do this.

## 5. Closes that convert (how the top ads end)

- **Endorsement wrapped around an instruction.** Billo's six-month CTR study: 74% of top-performing ads used a hard CTA, all five of the highest-CTR ads did, and the best were "direct instructions wrapped in personal endorsement" ("Trust me, go check out...", "I'll drop the link below if you want to try it yourself"). Generic "Link below" and "Check it out" underperformed (https://billo.app/creative-insider/inspiration-ideas/cta-examples/).
- **Name the person it is for, then the offer, then the risk reversal.** Myoovi: "If her period runs her calendar every month, this is the gift that actually changes it. Link's there, 60 days to try it."
- **Problem restated as the close.** Jones Road: "If you find yourself feeling aged when you put on any sort of foundation, you just need to change your foundation. I'll tag this one to this video." Spoonful of Comfort: "If you're looking to celebrate friends and family but can't be there, send them some spoonful of comfort today." Billo found problem-solution framing the strongest single CTA type.
- **Price as the last line.** Waterboy: "It's only $2 a stick at the link below."
- **Scarcity only if true.** Hismile: "Links below. The offer won't last forever."
- **Friend voice.** Fat Cow: "Don't say I never put you on. You're in on the secret now. Go glow." Drdent: "I'm going to stick them in the basket for you."
- **A question that invites a comment.** Ridge: "A lot of different colors to choose from. Which one are you going with?" Shortzly recommends "question callbacks, visual mirrors, or cliffhangers" so the ending earns a replay or reply (https://www.shortzly.com/blog/short-form-video-retention-strategies).
- **Spoken plus on-screen.** Koro's claim across 200+ accounts: clear spoken and visual direct-response CTAs beat passive endings by about 35% in ROAS; "link in bio" fails because it never answers "what's in it for me" (https://getkoro.app/blog/best-ugc-video-cta).
- **Gift framing where it fits naturally.** Billo: "for yourself or as a gift" doubled purchase occasions in unexpected categories. Myoovi and Cherish These are built on it.

## 6. What NOT to do

- **Talking head with a text overlay and nothing happening.** Opascope's 86 to 14 result against "voice-driven, no narrative arc, no b-roll" videos. OpusClip's static talking-head retention of 41% vs 58%. The face can open the video (VidMob), but the hands have to do something by second two.
- **Logo, brand card or "sponsored" disclaimer up top.** VidMob: logo up front with no story cut 6-second view-through 14%. Influencers Time: "front-loaded disclosures that read as dead stops" hurt retention. Shortzly: logos and self-promotion in the 3 to 15s window "kill 30-50% of viewers."
- **Polished studio look.** VidMob: everyday people hooked 1.7x more than celebrity talent; outdoor "shot on location" dropped 6s view-through 26%. Invideo: "phone-shot rather than professionally lit"; polished footage "signals advertisement." Ridge's studio title card m832232203292736 (15s, "INTRODUCING THE RIDGE POWERBANK") sits at rank 2 behind the handheld version m4401581883502460 at rank 1, same copy.
- **Generic hooks.** Billo's losing list: "You need to see this," "I found something amazing." OurOwnBrand: "Wait for it" style fake suspense (they cite a 23% first-3-seconds loss from a Reddit thread; treat the number as anecdotal).
- **A promise the body does not keep.** The high-hook low-hold pattern (Jetfuel, Sicard). Greenfroglabs notes that early engagement followed by early drop reads to the algorithm as bait-and-switch.
- **Pauses.** Shortzly: "Cut every pause longer than 0.3 seconds." OurOwnBrand: a pause over 1.2 seconds loses attention.
- **Static B-roll stretches over 3 to 4 seconds.** Influencers Time.
- **Too much text, too fast or too slow.** TikTok's guidance: 5 to 10 words per second when using text. OpusClip: captions inside 0.5 seconds because roughly 40% of first views play silent; OurOwnBrand cites Sked Social at 80%+ scroll with sound off.
- **Long, slow testimonials.** Ecology Skincare m1304571228116671 runs 143 seconds and sits at rank 1 of only 20 ads; Mums m511808595194639 runs 271 seconds. They work for small accounts with patient audiences, not as the creator-link default.
- **"Link in bio" with nothing else.** Koro and Billo above.
- **Staged family skits that look generated.** Cherish These m1446107257546440 is rank 1 and the frame looks synthetic. It runs because the text hook and the letter carry it, not because the footage is believable. Do not brief creators to copy the look.

## 7. How to keep them watching (paste onto every brand page)

Written for a creator. Plain words. One example each.

1. **Be doing something before you say anything.** The first frame should be your hands moving: dumping, pouring, scooping, shaking, opening. Faces alone get scrolled. Example: a finger dragging through the cream in the open jar, then the first line (Fat Cow m1807168496977543).

2. **Say the first line like you are halfway through a thought.** Start mid-task, mid-story, mid-argument. Example: "Just start by taking a little bit" while the brush is already in the pot (Jones Road m1564454271790169).

3. **Tell them who this is for inside the first sentence.** The right person leans in, the wrong person leaves, and that is fine. Example: "Boyfriends, you know how she always knows what you need? Here's how to return the favour." (Myoovi m3218042485041341).

4. **Promise something you will show later, and say when.** Example: "Let's pack Quinn's order and you'll see how we brought back the biggest free gift we've ever done." Then show it at 37 seconds (Hismile m1406603948018630).

5. **Change the picture every 3 to 5 seconds.** New angle, new prop, new place, new text. If you have been talking to the camera for 8 seconds without moving, cut. (OpusClip retention data; Socialync.)

6. **Go quiet when you test it.** Stop talking, let the camera watch the thing work, then react for real. Example: 20 silent seconds of the whitening strips, then "Look at that." (Drdent m1563635778876692).

7. **Raise your own doubt, then test it again.** "I reckon once I have a drink it'll go back to normal," rinse, check, "literally nothing's changed." (Drdent, same ad.) A second test is a second hook.

8. **Put the gripe in the middle, not at the start.** Open with the thing, bring the problem in once they trust you. Example: "I was getting so tired of my foundation making me look older" at 37 seconds (Jones Road).

9. **Bring out a second thing.** A second product, a comparison pan, a tracker card, a friend who disagrees. Example: "If you want more security, get the tracker card" at 14 seconds (Ridge m959241849840107).

10. **Use a count or a number that keeps changing.** "This has 3,100 milligrams." "This has 3,100 milligrams." Five rounds, then "Wait, it's just one drink." (Waterboy m1616399766694709.)

11. **End on the person and the instruction, in your own words.** Not "link below." Say who it is for, what to do, and why it is safe. Example: "If her period runs her calendar every month, this is the gift that actually changes it. Link's there, 60 days to try it." (Myoovi.)

12. **No logo, no "hey guys," no sponsored card up front.** The brand shows up in your hands, in the story, not as a title slide. (VidMob: a logo up front cut 6-second views 14%.)
