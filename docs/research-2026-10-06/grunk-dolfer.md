# Grunk Dolfer: October 2026 creator research (refresh of the September hub)

Written 2026-10-06 for the creator-link rebuild. Builds on `docs/angles-grunk-dolfer.md` (September research, 35 live angles). This doc does not repeat the brand pull, the seasonality table or the persona list from September; it adds what is working NOW in golf-apparel creator ads, what a creator should physically do on camera, and a rebuilt candidate list.

How this was gathered: Atria ad library (Bad Birdie, Bogey Bros, a library-wide sweep; the industry hook sweep returned only transcript noise and is not used), preview frames read by eye for the first-second description, free transcripts where Atria had them, and web research (creator trend reporting, brand sites, Trustpilot, Loox and on-site reviews, golf forums). Atria rate-limited hard for most of the session, so the category snapshot is narrower than planned: Good Good Golf, Waggle, Sunday Swagger, Swannies, Breezy, Birds of Condor, Devereux, Primo, William Murray, Eastside, Piss Missile, Chubbies, True Classic, Swing Juice and Golf Gods could not be resolved or searched in Atria this session (Good Good and Waggle returned `not_found`; the rest were never answered). What I know about them is from the web and is marked as such. Nothing below is invented; where I could not see something I say so.

No sales numbers in this doc. Everything here can go on the public link except the "our own ads" table in part 6.

## 2. Patterns (the shapes that keep showing up)

1. **The gift prank: how long until he notices.** Bogey Bros m1859794814679897 (rank 1, 192 days): woman narrates, hands over the polo, he says "Well, that's nice", then "Wait a second. Are those butt cheeks?". Also m2115617322635468 (a single text notification), m1754504355696010 ("DON'T LET HIM WEAR THIS TO YOUR PARENT'S HOUSE"). Why it works: the viewer knows something the man on screen does not, so they stay to watch him find out; the reveal is the print close-up, which is the product shot. The buyer is the woman, so the ad speaks to her.
2. **The forced deadpan review.** Bogey Bros m919515360698444 (rank 2): the woman's hand pushes him into frame, he side-eyes the camera and says one word, SOLID. Why: a man refusing to perform is funnier than a man performing, and the print sits still in frame for the whole clip.
3. **The partner objection as the headline.** "Are you letting your man wear this polo to dinner?" (m1447288833790481), "Would you let your man wear this polo?" (m1754504355696010), "Her mom probably won't notice the pattern anyway... right?" (m2115617322635468). Why: a question to the woman, about the man, with the shirt as the answer. It also pre-empts the "is it too much" objection by making it the joke.
4. **Unboxing that goes somewhere.** Bad Birdie m937514819007024 (kitchen, box, "FIRST LOOK: BAD BIRDIE'S NEW DROP") and m2116152639293586 (box opened on a range mat at night, "TAKING MY NEW BAD BIRDIE ITEMS TO THE RANGE"), both launched Oct 1 and rising. Why: the box promises a reveal, the location change at 3 to 5 seconds is the rehook, and the shirt is then shown in use rather than held up.
5. **Her voice, his polos.** Bad Birdie m1548498556597153 (rank 5): a polo flat on the bed, her hand on it, "PERFECT FIT, BUTTERY SOFT, HIS FAVORITE POLOS", body "He's golfing all the time... he might as well be best dressed." Why: the gift buyer hears a woman who already bought and is happy; the shot is tactile (hand on fabric) which sells "soft" without a claim.
6. **Play bad, look good.** Bad Birdie "Below average golfer? At least be the best dressed." (m2238178400364685, two copies, one rising) and Bogey Bros' mailer bag PLAY BAD. LOOK GOOD. Why: it removes the fear of looking like a try-hard. You are allowed the shirt because you are not pretending to be good. Grunk's site already says it better: "We specialize in looking amazing while sucking at the game we all love."
7. **Not for try-hards (the reverse listicle).** Bogey Bros m2108429963090261: polos on a garage rail, "3 reasons why you should NEVER buy bogey bros", body "not for golf hardos, try-hards, those who can't take a joke". Why: a negative hook is a pattern break in a feed of "you need this", and it lets the viewer self-select as the fun one.
8. **The regular-guy authority.** Bogey Bros' Geno Bonnalie (PGA Tour caddie) in a cart with a buddy in matching polos: "my wife goes 'Oh I really like that!' and then I said, 'But, do you get it?'" (m1228261266174271, rank 4). Why: a caddie is a pro who is not a pro golfer; he drinks beer and wears the shirt. Grunk's version is Nick, or a TRYBE creator who is visibly a 15-handicap.
9. **The foursome walking shot with banter.** birddogs m1138217752221924 (rank 1 of 463, Sep 30): four guys walking a fairway, one line of dialogue in subtitles, body "who invited the camera guy?". Why: the group is the product context; the viewer joins the group. This is the gap the September doc flagged ("No faces, no friends, no round") and it is now a top ad for a competitor.
10. **Come play two holes with me.** Bad Birdie m1623760625766692: woman on the tee, "Play 2 holes with me". Why: an invitation plus a built-in structure (hole 1, hole 2) that promises an ending. The outfit is seen walking, swinging, in a cart, not held up.
11. **The one-line text message.** Bogey Bros m2115617322635468 is a black screen with a single iMessage notification: "DO NOT WEAR THAT SHIRT TO MY MOM'S HOUSE !!!!!". Ten seconds, rank 7. Why: it reads like your own phone. Note the September doc found our meme-wrapper ads lost (fake tweet 0.86, iMessage 1.12); the difference is Bogey Bros' text is a real-sounding line from a real-sounding person, and the shirt follows immediately. Treat as a test, not a bet.
12. **Short product clip, nothing else.** Manors Golf's 8-second rank-1 ad. Why: for a clean product with a strong look, the shortest possible loop beats a script. Grunk's version is the print filling the frame (already proven by the 287-12 still and the Tiktok #6 cut).

What reads relatable vs try-hard in 2026 (web):
- FrontPorchDad (Tyler Jarry, 900K+): "A lot of the times it's not even stuff that I think is hilarious, it's just stuff that I hear people say a lot." His "Bad Golf Shots" reel is just lines golfers say: "I knew I should have used my 6!", "Hit a house!", "I just yanked it.", "Fore!" (golf.com). Brand deals followed (Rhoback, PGA Superstore, Titleist, FootJoy).
- Birdogey's brand is "the self-aware, perpetually optimistic weekend golfer who knows exactly how the round is going to end" (creatordb).
- Bob Does Sports' humour is catchphrases earned over time ("Have a day", "Bobby Fairways" after zero fairways in 36 holes). Borrowed catchphrases read as try-hard; earned ones do not.
- Golf forums on loud clothes: "There's nothing wrong with loud as long as there is some style with it." / "I just get bored of the beige chinos / navy shirt lot." / "I'm buying stuff more suited to a funeral." (Golf Monthly). "It's not the clothes. It's the giant beer gut, and it's the fit of the loud clothes." / "Worst dressed does not mean 'flashiest' or 'most colorful,' but rather it means 'unkempt.'" / "You can wear loud clothes and look good." (The Sand Trap). Australian Golf Digest's hate list is about mismatch and chaos ("looked like a Vegas driving-range hustler"), not colour.
- The rule that falls out: loud is fine, performing is not. Say the thing people actually say, let the print do the joke, keep the shirt fitting (which is Grunk's relaxed cut) and tucked or clean. Acting drunk, forced catchphrases, shouting at the lens and reading the product page are the try-hard tells.
