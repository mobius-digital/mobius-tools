# Party Patch: creator-link research (2026-10-06)

Raw material for the rebuild of tools.go-mobius-digital.com/angles/party-patch. Everything below was seen on Atria (ad ids m...), on the web (URL given) or in our own Meta account (ad ids given in the brief). Nothing is invented. Where I could not confirm something it is in part 7.

A note on the Atria session: the connector rate-limited most calls today (another agent was on the same workspace). I got about 23 search / resolve / tag calls through and 18 free transcripts. Bytox and DrinkAde (US) are not in the library, Flyby has one ad, More Labs never resolved (every attempt was throttled), and the industry-wide hook sweep returned transcription junk ("You", "Thank you.", "We'll be right back."), so the category hooks below come from the advertiser pages, not the sweep. The persona sweep did work and is in part 1.

The short version: in this category the creator videos that get impressions are not people talking about hangovers. They are two-person trades, a real day with the product riding along, label reads, six-second loops, honest "not a cure" openers, and persona call-outs ("if you have kids"). Three brands launched or are running Party Patch's exact "tomorrow is still happening" idea this quarter (Get 1906 on Oct 1, dayguard since July, ZBiotics since September), and the Cheers founder says out loud that his buyers are "moms and dads and grandparents" with "kids that are waking up early". The persona is right. The TRYBE videos lost because of their shape, not their audience.

## 2. Patterns: the shapes that keep showing up

1. **The trade.** Two people alternate one-line claims; one holds a pile, the other holds one thing; the ending flips to price. Waterboy m1616399766694709, m1058963726969933 (both partner, both high rank, the second rising). Why it works: every line is a tiny question the viewer predicts the answer to, so the rhythm holds them; the pile is a visual joke that needs no words; the last line is a price reveal, which is the only moment the ad "sells".
2. **The honest anti-claim opener.** "Waterboy is not a cure for your hangover." (m2432062563971120, hook tag; copy "Again, not a cure."); Cheers "Plot Twist: I make money telling people they SHOULDN'T drink" (m1013483928135810). Why: it breaks the ad pattern in the first second, it earns trust, and for us it is the compliance line said as a hook instead of a disclaimer.
3. **A real day, product riding along.** Waterboy mom m1386099517035759 (Disneyland, beach, red-eye, product at 0:29), Waterboy pregnancy m912058048217135, ZBiotics bachelorette m1025728377183837 (Pilates the next morning). Why: the first 10 seconds are a story the viewer would watch without a product; the persona is shown, not described; the product is the answer to a situation already on screen.
4. **Tonight / tomorrow in one breath.** Waterboy "tonight's gonna be amazing tomorrow is gonna suck unless" (m1541318970835567), dayguard "Drink Tonight. Feel Better Tomorrow." (m1720332875988351, 140 days), Get 1906 "Off Duty on Friday. On duty for Saturday's 7am pancakes." (m1859426501710827). Why: the stake (the morning) is stated in under three seconds, and the viewer fills in her own tomorrow. This is Chris's idea, and three brands are running it.
5. **The label read / comparison.** "i'm 35 years old and i just found out what's in these" (m3128643533989877), the bartender math (m2060805071981201), "Check the label" (m961104253707387). Why: age plus "just found out" is a curiosity gap; numbers on screen give the eye something to do; the viewer feels she is doing the comparing.
6. **The persona call-out in line one.** "If you drink alcohol and you have kids" (Cheers m1367112002210185), "Are you a guy, over 30, make $100K+" (m1530114918595086), "40+ and feeling like your energy isn't what it used to be?" (Good Patch m1775730420219793, rising). Why: self-selection. The person described stops; everyone else scrolls, which is fine.
7. **The product personified / the quiz.** "Do I help with nausea? Yeah... What am I?" (Waterboy m2202458353636113). Why: a yes/no run is a rhythm, the reveal is the brand name, and it is funny without a joke.
8. **The six-second loop.** The Good Patch m1472651414618482 (6.8s, rank 1 for 80 days), m1403325145304466 (6.9s), ZBiotics m28182952651327819 (3.9s). Why: the action (peel, stick, go) IS the product demo and it plays three times before a talking head finishes saying hello. Cheap to make in five versions.
9. **The cultural curiosity question.** "Why do Koreans never get hangovers?" (dayguard m27797344126635112, rank 1), "Do you know anybody who turns red when they drink?" (Cheers m1358429773117200). Why: the question is interesting even if you never buy.
10. **The absurd premise.** Rent-a-waterboy (m1401112791381628, 82 seconds, rising), "Gam Gam is fine" (m1594813695513587). Why: comedy earns the full watch; the product is the punchline, not the pitch.
11. **The review read aloud.** Waterboy reads "Cali D said for a recent bachelorette party I included Waterboy in hangover kits I made" (m1541318970835567). Why: social proof in a customer's words, and the review supplies a scene (kits) the creator can act out.
12. **The morning described in body parts.** "Your head's splitting, your face is puffy, and your stomach's churning. This is your precious weekend, but you're wasting it, rotting" (dayguard m1533442648531847). Why: specific physical words; but note it shows the bad morning, which our page bans. Use the words, not the picture.

What does NOT show up among the winners: a person on a couch putting a patch on and explaining hangovers to camera with a text overlay. The one Waterboy ad closest to that shape opens "Do you ever just wake up feeling so hungover" and sits at rank 30 of 91.

## 3. Visual hooks and rehooks seen

Physical openers (what is on screen at 0s):

- A kitchen counter covered in Pedialyte, milk, oranges, capsules and ginger, one hand sets down a single stick (Waterboy m1616399766694709; copy spells out the pile).
- Two people face to face, each holding a different thing, first line said before any logo (m1058963726969933).
- A woman holding the product up like a puppet and asking it questions (m2202458353636113).
- A mom talking fast to the front camera in a car or hotel room, kids in the background, no product for 29 seconds (m1386099517035759).
- A bride-to-be clip reel: dancing on tables, then a Pilates studio (ZBiotics m1025728377183837).
- A hand flipping two packs over to the ingredient panel (m3128643533989877, m961104253707387).
- A bartender behind a bar pouring, product on the bar (m2060805071981201, first line "Let's see how it tastes").
- Inside of the wrist, patch pressed on, camera close (The Good Patch m1775730420219793, m1472651414618482).
- A woman in a planner pose with a drink, text "Happy hour for control freaks" (Get 1906 m1061582806703549).
- Pancakes and a kid at 7am after a bar clip (Get 1906 m1859426501710827 per its copy; I could not see the frame, the copy states the two scenes).
- A snoozed alarm and a face in a pillow (dayguard m1533442648531847, the banned picture for us).
- Someone sweating in Austin heat asking strangers for "a waterboy" (m1401112791381628).

Rehooks (what happens at 3 to 10 seconds that keeps them):

- The second person answers the claim and the camera cuts to them (the trade).
- A price line arrives after a list ("That must have cost a fortune." "$2 a stick.").
- The yes/no run speeds up ("Yeah." "Yeah." "Dude, yes.").
- "Wait, it's just one drink." (a reveal that collapses the pile into one thing).
- A customer review read in the creator's own voice, with the name ("Cali D said...").
- The flip to the ingredient panel with numbers on screen.
- "Let's see how it tastes" followed by the actual taste at the end (a promise with a payoff two minutes later).
- "It comes down to three things. Number one..." (a count the viewer stays for).
- A hard cut from night to morning with a time on screen (Off Duty / On duty, Tonight / Tomorrow).
- The product arriving late (0:29) after the story has already earned the watch.
- "Not a cure" followed immediately by "but" and what it is.
