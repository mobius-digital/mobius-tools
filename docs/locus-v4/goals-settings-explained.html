Goals and ad rules, explained

What every section on the screen does today, the answers to your questions, what is broken, and the simpler screen it should become.

How the pieces fit

Four levels, top to bottom. Each one only decides its own level. The highlighted one is what the "judged on" switch sets.

1. The brand's goalHome > Goals (the plan)

Revenue and MER for the month (2.5x floor, Grunk 3.0x). Whole store, every channel. This is "is the business winning". The ad settings never change it.

feeds
2. The ad goalBrand settings > Goals and ad rules

ONE measure (CPA, CAC, ROAS or CPL) and one number. It is what an ad set must hit to be kept or scaled. Same goal on every ad platform unless you set a platform goal.

optional override
3. Platform goalsame screen

Replaces level 2 on one platform only, in the same measure. Needed because Meta, Google and TikTok each give themselves credit differently.

used by
4. The callsAds > Today's calls

Every live Meta ad set judged daily (Scale, Cut, Trim, Refresh, Mixed). New numbered tests judged once (Winner, Keep, Loser). Google and TikTok have no daily calls; their goals feed the leak finder.

Your questions

"Goals and break-even at a glance": what is highlighted, and where do the numbers come from?

The highlighted row is the measure the calls use (it says "used for the calls"). Bonk shows cost per sale because nobody ever picked a measure, and cost per sale is the default.

  • Goal: the number we typed in "The goals" below. On Oct 15 we set every brand to 90-day AOV divided by 2.5 ROAS (Bonk $25).
  • Break-even: AOV times what an order keeps after product, shipping and fees (Triple Whale costs, last 90 days).
  • Real: the last 90 days, whole store, Triple Whale: ad spend divided by orders (or by new customers, or MER for ROAS).

The trap: "Real" is whole-store, but the calls judge Meta's own credited sales, which run about 2x worse. Bonk's real looks fine while Meta shows about $58 a sale, so the calls keep saying Cut.

"What we agreed with the client": can it change?

Yes, any time, in "The goals" section. Nothing is locked. The words mean "agree this number with the client before you change it", not that it is frozen. The at-a-glance table is just a read-only view of it.

Why "cost per sale" and not CPA? Why not CAC?

It was written in plain words on purpose. You are right that it reads worse: the team says CPA and CAC. The fix is the real term as the label with one line of definition under it (CPA, cost per any order; CAC, cost per new customer).

"What this brand is judged on": is that the overall brand goal?

No. It is only the measure the AD calls use. The overall brand goal is the MER plan on Home > Goals. The section name is the problem; it should say "Ad goal".

"The goals · Every platform": does each ad platform get its own goal?

No. "Every platform" means the one number applies to Meta, Google and TikTok alike. Yes, ROAS 2.5 there means "every platform should run at 2.5" unless you set a platform goal.

I fill in ROAS 2.5 AND CAC $20, but "judged on" is ROAS. What is used?

The calls use ROAS 2.5. CAC $20 is not used by any call; it only shows on reports and the goals table.

One exception, built Oct 21: goal CPA and goal ROAS also act as guardrails when they are not the main measure. If you judge on CPA and ROAS is filled in, a set over the CPA goal whose ROAS beats the ROAS goal is NOT cut; it shows as Mixed for you to decide. That is exactly your "great ROAS, why turn it off" case. CAC and CPL are never guardrails.

None of this is visible on the screen today. It is buried in one sentence of the Cut rule.

A goal per platform: does Meta's replace the one above?

Yes, for that platform only, in the same measure. Meta's number drives Today's calls. Google and TikTok numbers only feed the leak finder, since there are no Google or TikTok calls.

The "$" you saw after switching to ROAS is a bug: the box picks its unit when the page opens and never updates. Worse, a saved Meta goal of 40 would silently become 40x ROAS if the measure changes.

"Ad calls as sentences" vs "Test calls" vs "Test minimums"
  • Ad calls: every live Meta ad set, every day. Should it get more money, less, or be turned off? Scale, Cut, Trim, Refresh, Mixed.
  • Test calls: a NEW numbered test (batch 415 etc.), judged once, so the team learns. Winner, Keep running, Loser, with a learning written down.
  • Test minimums: a forced daily spend on a new test's ad set so Meta gives it a fair start; Locus tells you in Slack when to take it off (7 days, or once judged).

The sentences with number boxes inside them read badly. A table with one row per call is clearer.

Where are TikTok and Google settings? Does Google need calls?

The same rules can apply to TikTok once it is connected directly; today it is not, so there are no TikTok calls. Settings should stay one set of rules for every platform, not a copy per platform.

Google for e-commerce is judged differently: by campaign (Search, Shopping, Performance Max) on ROAS, plus search terms to block. There are no ad-level "cut this ad" calls like Meta. The leak finder already covers it (wasted search terms, campaigns under goal). So no Google call rules are needed; the Google platform goal is all it needs.
