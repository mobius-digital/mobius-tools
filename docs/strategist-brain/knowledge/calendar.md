# The marketing calendar: drops, sales and the work behind them

Scope: what customers see and when (drops, sales and offers, ad pushes, site changes, the Black Friday plan, email and text sends), how each date drives the work that has to be ready for it, how to space promotions, and how to judge a date after it ran. Read with `offers-pricing.md` (what the offer should be), `stock.md` (whether the product can carry the push), `email-sms.md` (send cadence) and `cross-channel.md` (lags).

The rule behind this file: **a date that sits in a box does nothing.** Every drop or sale starts a countdown (photos in, ads briefed, ads built, the email scheduled, the ads loaded). The calendar's job is to make that countdown visible and late steps loud, and after the date to say whether it worked.

---

## 1. How it works now (Locus, October 2026)

- **One list, four sources, nothing typed twice.** The `calendar` view merges: dates typed in Locus or by a client in the old Lineup app (the same rows), the Black Friday plan's phases (offers, read-only, edited on the season page), Products > Drops on-site dates, and Klaviyo email and text campaigns, scheduled and sent.
- **Kinds:** Drop or launch, Sale or offer, Ad push, Site change, Other (teasers, list builds, giveaways and contests). Status: confirmed or pencilled; a Black Friday phase with no offer is "no offer yet".
- **The countdown per kind** (days before going live): drop = photos in -14 (or the client's own asset date), ads briefed -12, built -5, email and text scheduled -3, ads loaded -2. Sale = offer written -21, briefed -14, built -5, email -3, loaded -2. Black Friday phases use the season plan's own rule: offer locked -30, briefed -23, built -9, loaded -4.
- **It ticks itself.** Photos = a link on the date. Offer = written and confirmed (season: locked). Briefed and built = the Asana task made from the date is completed ("Make the Asana tasks" creates one task per step in the brand's project, due on the step's date). Email = a Klaviyo email or text scheduled within a day of going live. Ads loaded = new ads created in the brand's Meta accounts in the 10 days before. A person can also tick a step by hand. Once a date has gone live it has no countdown.
- **Late** = a step whose due date has passed and is not done. "Work due this week" on the Calendar page lists every step due in the next seven days plus anything late, grouped.
- **Slack** (each brand's internal channel): a ping when a client adds or moves a date; the day before every date (one line when everything is done); a week before only when something is still open; and on Mondays "still running?" for a sale with no end date.
- **On the charts:** each drop and sale draws as a shaded band on Home's revenue chart, so a jump and its cause sit together.

## 2. Decision rules

### Spacing and stacking
- **Leave at least six weeks between big sitewide sales** on the same brand. Closer than that teaches the list to wait for the next one; conversion at full price drops in between. A drop at full price does not count as a sale.
- **Never run two discounts at once** on the same brand unless one is a contest or a gift with purchase. Two discounts stack in the customer's head as "always on sale".
- **Give every promotion one job:** acquire new customers, lift order size, clear stock, or reward the list. A promotion with two jobs does neither; name the job when you suggest one.
- **A sale needs an end date.** An open-ended discount becomes the new price. If a sale has no end, say so and propose one (usually the day before the next planned offer).
- **A new drop within a week of Black Friday** must be decided: in the Black Friday offer or excluded. Decide it 30 days out so the briefs and the site say it.

### Lead times
- Creative needs three to six weeks for a real launch: photos two weeks before, briefs about 12 days before, ads built five days before. Under ten days of notice, cut scope (fewer ads, reuse proven formats) rather than miss the date.
- The email and text must be scheduled three days before; the ads loaded two days before so they clear review.
- For Black Friday the season plan's longer rule applies (offer locked 30 days out).

### Stock
- Before confirming a drop or a sale on a product, read `stock`: a sale on a product that runs out first wastes the spend and the list's attention. A drop whose stock lands after the on-site date needs pre-order wording.

### Judging a date after it ran
- Compare the date's days with the same number of days before it (revenue, orders, order size, ad spend, MER), then look at the same window after it. A sale that tripled revenue and was followed by a week 30% under normal pulled demand forward; count the dip against the lift.
- A drop is judged on new-customer orders and the products in the drop, not on total revenue alone.

## 3. Diagnostics

- **Revenue jumped or fell on a day:** check the calendar first. A sale starting, a drop going live, an email send or a sale ending explains most single-day moves before any ad change does.
- **A date with no countdown progress a week out:** no Asana task was made, or nobody completed it. Say which step and who owns it (the call sheet names the strategist, buyer and email owner).
- **"Nothing planned" for a client for six weeks** in Q4 is a finding: ask the client what is coming, or propose a date.
- **Two offers overlap:** read both; if both are discounts, recommend ending or moving one.
- **A sale with no end date running more than a week:** propose the end, and say what the open discount does to full-price demand.

## 4. Effects on the other channels

- **Meta:** a sale lifts conversion rate within a day; CPA drops while it runs and rises the week after as the warm audience has bought. Plan budget up on day one, down the day after it ends, and do not judge new creative launched during a sale against normal weeks.
- **Email and SMS:** each sale needs a launch, a reminder and a last call; three sends in a week is normal around a sale and too many outside one. A drop to the list first (24 to 48 hours early) rewards subscribers and makes the public launch day stronger.
- **Google:** brand search rises with a sale or drop announcement; branded campaigns need budget headroom on those days.
- **Site:** the offer must be on the site (banner, discount live, sale page tested) the day before; a missing banner costs more than a weak ad.

## 5. Worked suggestions

- "Grunk's Burgundy tiers (15/20/25%) have no end date and would run into the Black Friday BOGO on Nov 27. End them Nov 25 so the BOGO lands fresh; the tiers had six weeks, long enough."
- "Splatter goes live Oct 23 and its briefs were due Oct 11 with no Asana task: make the tasks today and cut it to three ads from proven formats, since the full set cannot be built in eight days."
- "Dartee's Warehouse Sale (Sep 3 to 10) made 3x the revenue of the eight days before; the eight days after came in 31% under, partly because spend was cut 34%. The next sale is the Early Bird on Nov 1, seven and a half weeks later: spaced well."
- "SpeedIn and VetriPaws have nothing on the calendar for six weeks. Ask both in their client channels what is coming before Black Friday."

## 6. Traps

- **A typed date is only as good as the person who typed it.** Grunk's Burgundy sat at Oct 7 in the calendar while the plan said Oct 13; check a date against the brief and the client channel when they disagree.
- **Klaviyo campaign names carry a filing prefix** ("REM - Email - FRI 10/09/2026 - ..."); the part after the date is what the email is.
- **Ads loaded ticks on any new ad in the account** in the ten days before, not only the ads for that date. Treat it as a strong hint, not proof.
- **The Black Friday plan's phases are read-only here.** Change them on the season page (open=season), never by adding a duplicate calendar date.
- **Client edits still come through the old Lineup** until clients sign in to Locus; a client's new or moved date pings the brand's internal channel.
