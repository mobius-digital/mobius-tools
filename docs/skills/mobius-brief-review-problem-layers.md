## Paste 2: references/checklist.md, a new section after "Strategy"

```
## Problem layers (StoryBrand)
- [ ] We can say what the customer WANTS, and the villain is the thing in the way (a thing, never a person or a named competitor)
- [ ] The story holds together: the villain causes the problem, the problem causes the feeling
- [ ] The layer fits who sees it. Cold audience and only features or specs is our most common miss; warm or retargeting traffic wants proof, offer and guarantee
- [ ] The feeling is one real moment in the customer's words, not "feel confident"
- [ ] Any "should / shouldn't have to" line rejects a real norm the customer already resents, and this product can back it up. A slogan ("your belt should work as hard as your bag") fails
- [ ] Status, fun and gift products: it argues the payoff (the compliment, access, scarcity, who they become), not a forced problem
- [ ] The brand's known problems (Locus, Brand > Research, The problem) are a map, not a rule. A different villain is fine if the story holds
```

## Paste 3: references/framework.md, under the 3-question test

```
Problem layers and labels:
- A different want, villain or layer that the WHOLE ad argues = a new angle (the reason to buy changed).
- Same story, only the first line opens on a different layer = a hook test inside the concept.
- Miller's seven parts are a menu of truly different concepts under one angle: the villain as a character, the moment, the proof, the plan or the fear answered, the stakes (a pinch), the happy ending, the before and after of who they become. Three concepts should usually come from three different parts.
```
