The 4-4-4 day you saw

It is Alex Hormozi, in a Short called "Time Schedule For New Entrepreneurs". Three 4-hour blocks, in this order:

1. PromoteContent, outreach, ads. Getting attention.
2. BuildMake the product or the offer better.
3. DeliverFulfil, serve customers, follow up.

My take. The order is the gold: promotion goes first because it is the thing delivery always pushes out. But he built it for someone with one business. Run it daily across two companies and you switch company and mode three or four times a day, which is exactly how you feel now. So keep his order, and apply it inside a company day, not across the whole day.

Five rules

  1. One company per block, one kind of work per block.A Lucky block never opens Mobius Slack, and the reverse.
  2. Calls only in two windows.Monday afternoon for every client call. Thursday afternoon for everything else. The 8am team meeting is the only morning call.
  3. Media buying is one daily pass, every brand at once.Lucky ads included. Buying is one skill, so it is one sitting off the Buyer list, then done.
  4. The gym sits between the morning and afternoon, 10 to 12.Two hours so it never gets squeezed. Breakfast at 6:30, lunch at 12, dinner after 5:30.
  5. Friday plans the next week, so Monday never starts cold.Every block for next week already knows what it holds before the weekend.

Whole days or half days?

Whole days. A half-day split means you change companies every single day at noon, and the morning company follows you into the afternoon (that is the residue the research talks about). Lucky work is making things: ads, pages, product. It needs a run of hours to get momentum, and a 3-hour morning ends right as you get going.

The one thing that touches both every day is the 5:30 Buy hour, because ads cannot wait a week. Mobius Slack gets read at Start and at Shutdown, not in between. So Lucky gets Tuesday and Thursday morning, Mobius gets Monday and Wednesday, and Friday catches whichever slipped.

How a task gets into a block

You say it

Locus files it

It becomes a task on your list with the company, the block it belongs in (Lucky Build), a rough size, and the due date.

Locus places it

It goes into the first matching block before the due date that has room: Tuesday afternoon. That block's calendar event is rewritten right away with its list.

If no block has room in time, Locus tells you in one line and asks what to bump.

5am card

The morning card shows today's two blocks and what is in each. You open the block, the list is there.

Friday

The Plan hour shows what did not get done, rolls it forward, and asks for each company's top 3 for next week.

Can the blocks repeat every week if the notes change?

Yes. Google lets one day of a repeating event carry its own notes without touching the rest of the series. So each block is set up once as a repeating event, and Locus only rewrites that day's notes. You get the same calendar every week, with different contents.
