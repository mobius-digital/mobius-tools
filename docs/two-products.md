## 1. The video, read closely

Fifty three seconds, made with Claude and Remotion (the code is on screen at the end; "Tally" there is a concept,
not a product you can buy). The script, beat by beat:

1. "Your dashboard is lying to you." It says you made $1,284.
2. "You actually kept $412." The number melts into a puddle. The puddle gets eyes. (sigh)
3. "Where did the rest go?" Fees ate $164. Ads burned $398. Refunds took $96. And shipping? $214.
   Each cost is a character (a purple blob, a blue flame, a pink spiral, a cardboard box) that takes a bite.
4. "And you only find out at 11:47, midnight, 14 tabs open."
5. Tally. "Every store. Every payout. Every ad account. Melted into one number." $412.00, circled. "The real one."
6. "When your ads start bleeding, it taps you on the shoulder." A nudge card. "One swipe. Paused."
7. "Every morning, 7:02, before coffee, you know exactly where you stand." A phone, one sentence.
8. "Fewer tabs. More sleep."

What makes it work, in order of importance: one number is the hero and everything else is a bite out of it;
every cost has a face; the product appears only after the pain, and it does three things (one number, a tap on
the shoulder, a morning line); one bold color field; a serif italic on the single word that matters ("actually",
"Paused.", "More sleep."); the mascot reacts instead of talking. Nothing in it is a feature list.

And the uncomfortable part: that spot is the exact pain Locus was started for, and its three promises are already
half built across our two apps. The video is the pitch we never wrote.

## 2. What each product is

### The money app (Mobius Ledger, Lucky Ledger, Tally today)

- **The one line.** The money truth for a small business: what came in, what you actually kept, what is coming,
  and the books your CPA needs, kept clean every night by a helper that taps you on the shoulder.
- **Who.** The owner. One person, a few times a week, two minutes. Three editions: a service business (an
  agency, a consultant), a product brand (a store), a household. The CPA reads the export. Nobody else logs in.
- **The job.** Monthly truth and the future: the real profit and loss from the bank, cash in the bank and the next
  13 weeks, what you owe, taxes set aside, the month closed, the pack for the CPA.
- **The 30 seconds.** "Your bank balance is not your profit. The money app reads every account, every sale and
  every bill, and tells you one number every night: what you actually kept. It sees the bill that did not arrive,
  the payment that landed twice, the ad spend creeping past a share of sales, and it taps you on the shoulder.
  Close the month in one sitting. Hand your CPA a pack, not a shoebox."

### Locus

- **The one line.** Where a brand's growth gets run: every store, every ad account, every email, read every
  morning, with the calls to make today.
- **Who.** A brand owner who runs ads, and the agency that runs ads for many brands. Inside the agency: the
  owner, the media buyer, the creative strategist, the editor, the client. Every page in Locus has one of those
  five names on it (docs/locus-hub/product-map.md).
- **The job.** Daily truth and the next move: is the spend working, what do I change today, what do we make next,
  what did we send the client, what is on the calendar.
- **The 30 seconds.** "Meta says 4.1. Shopify says 2.3. Fourteen tabs and nobody knows what to change. Locus reads
  every store, every ad account and every email before you wake up, writes the morning brief, and puts the calls
  on one list: scale this, cut that, refresh this creative. One tap and it is done and logged. The creative engine
  tells you what to make next from what has already won. For an agency, every brand, one morning."

### Against the apps people already know

| | Triple Whale | Hyros | Locus |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| What it is | the dashboard: blended numbers, attribution, a daily summary | attribution: which ad really made the sale | the morning read and the calls, with the team acting inside it |
| Tells you what to change | no, it shows numbers | no | yes, ranked by money at stake, one tap to do it |
| Creative | reports on ads | no | what to make next, from what won; the brief; the library |
| Many brands at once | one workspace per brand | per account | the agency edition: every brand, one morning |
| Pitch in one breath | "see your real numbers" | "trust your attribution" | "know what to change today" |

Locus should never pitch against Triple Whale on attribution. It reads Triple Whale. It pitches on the call.

### Is Locus too extravagant?

For the team, no. Every page answers a question a named reader asks, and the restructure on October 9 already
cut the fat. For the pitch, yes. Nobody can hold eleven rail groups in their head. The rule: the pitch names three
things only (the morning read, today's calls, the creative engine) and the rest is "and the team works in it".
Calendar, Season, Brand, Products and Tools stay in the product and never appear in a sentence that sells it.

### The boundary between the two

| | Locus | the money app |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Side of the business | growth: what the platforms say, daily | money: what the bank says, monthly |
| Its numbers | sales, spend, MER, contribution margin, by channel and ad | profit and loss, cash, debts, taxes, the close |
| Its clock | every morning | every night, every Monday, month end |
| Its helper | Locus (the Strategist) | the money helper (Controller, Caddie, Tally today) |

The one shared number is "what you actually kept". It is the money app's number, because only the bank knows
it. Locus shows it on Home when a brand uses both apps, labeled as the money app's number, and keeps calling its
own figure contribution margin. The apps never merge. One reads the other.

## 3. The name

"Ledger" has to go: Ledger is a large crypto hardware wallet brand, and "ledger" is also the word QuickBooks uses
for the boring part. "Tally" is taken three ways (this video, Tally.so forms, TallyPrime accounting). Three
options, none checked for trademark, searched for obvious clashes only:

- **Kept.** The word from the one line that matters: what you actually kept. Four letters, says the job, works for
  all three editions ("Kept for a household"). Clashes found: a Thai bank's savings app and a one-person iOS
  budget tracker, both small. Domain likely needs a modifier (keptbooks, getkept, kept.money).
- **Till.** The cash drawer. "What is in the till." Warm, old, plain. Clash: Till Financial, a kids' debit card.
- **Keel.** What keeps the boat upright. Strong feeling, no money meaning on its own; needs a tagline every time.

Recommendation: **Kept**. The commercial writes itself ("Know what you kept"), the helper's line writes itself
("Kept taps you on the shoulder"), and it pairs with Locus: Locus knows where you are, Kept knows what you have.

## 4. Personality: the helper has a name and a face

Today the helpers are words in a settings page: Ask Locus (the Strategist) and the Controller, the Caddie, Tally.
The video's lesson is that the character carries the brand. One character per product, drawn the same way:

- **Locus: the dot.** A locus is a point that moves along a path; the mark is a glowing blue point with a trail.
  It lives in the ask bar, blinks while thinking, lights up when it has a call for you, goes dim when all is well.
  Its name is Locus. "Ask Locus" stays.
- **Kept: the coin.** A round green coin with a face; it gets heavier when the month is good and thinner when
  money leaks; it sighs at a loss and nods at a clean close. Its name is Kept. Each edition may give it a
  nickname in Settings (Lucky keeps "the Caddie"), the way you name a car. One drawing, one voice.
- **Shared rules.** The character never talks in paragraphs; it taps you on the shoulder with one sentence and one
  button. It never blocks a screen. It has four states (asleep, thinking, found something, all clear) and that is
  all. It appears in the gate, the ask bar, the nudge card, the empty states, and the morning line.
- **Logos.** Locus gets its own mark (the dot and its path), Kept gets the coin, the Mobius mark leaves the apps and
  stays on the agency site. Both marks share one construction (a circle, one stroke weight, the app's color) so
  they read as a family.
