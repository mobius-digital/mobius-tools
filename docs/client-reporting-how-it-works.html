Locus, client channels

How the client reporting loop works

Two posts a week-day, both from Cole, both built from numbers and from what a person typed. Locus writes no sentence a client reads.

The week, start to finish

  1. Every morning, 5amLocus pulls yesterday from Triple Whale and Meta and posts the daily to each client channel as Cole. No daily on a Monday or the 1st: the report covers that day.
  2. Monday, 5amThe weekly draft posts to #daily-briefs, numbers only. Nothing goes to the client yet.
  3. Monday, 8am callThe strategist reads the week in their own words and types the batches, the jobs and the asks on the call screen (Work > Calls). Those typed lines are the only words on the weekly.
  4. Finish on the call screenBatches become numbered briefs in Asana and Production cards. Asks go into Requests with a date. "This week" lands on the weekly draft. The draft card in #daily-briefs is redrawn with the client text.
  5. Monday, after the callCole presses Send on the card. The weekly goes to the client channel. If nobody presses it, nothing goes.
  6. Tuesday to SundayDailies only. A buyer who wants a line under the numbers types it in the brief's note box; it shows as Next for two days. Fireflies reads the call afterwards and adds anything said and not typed, as a card to approve, never to the client.

The rule on channels

The top post never names a channel. The thread names every ad channel that spent that day, biggest first, with the sales Triple Whale tracked to it. Under two tracked orders the rate is withheld ("too few tracked orders to rate yet"). Attribution not synced yet: spend only. Meta and Google appear because the agency runs ads there; TikTok joins the same way the day it spends.

Personality, and where it lives

Locus does not write voice. A machine that tries to sound like the team sounds like a machine, and the client learns to skim it. So the posts are plain and the same every day, and the personality lives in the three places a person types: the daily's Next, the weekly's This week, and the asks. A good week gets a good sentence because the numbers earned it, not because a model felt like it. When something deserves a joke or an emoji, the strategist types it in Next, and that is the line the client reads first.

What changed in this pass

  • The daily lost its AI headline ("Meta returning 3.24 per dollar"). The title is "Daily update"; the date sits on the Yesterday line.
  • The thread is numbers only. The AI narrative (What we saw, What it means, What we're doing) no longer reaches a client.
  • The weekly sentence is written by a rule from the two numbers that moved, not by a model.
  • "Profit" is "Contribution margin", defined under the number.
  • "This week" only takes calendar dates in the next 7 days (Nov 1 no longer shows on Oct 19).
  • Asks carry when they were asked and when they are needed.
