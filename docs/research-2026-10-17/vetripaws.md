# VetriPaws creator link: video research (2026-10-17)

VetriPaws' own Meta videos have almost no spend, so this board is built from other brands. Every pick was downloaded and watched frame by frame (contact sheet every 1.5s). The other brand's product does not matter; what we copy is the format, and every pick can be redone by one person with a phone, a dog and a VetriPaws strip.

## Where I looked (Atria, Meta library, active since June 2026)
- Dog supplements: Dog is Human (biggest UGC library in the category, 3,700+ ads), PetLab Co., Native Pet, Wuffes, Zesty Paws, Petsmont, Ruff Greens, Jope, Nutri Paw, Dinovite, Different Dog, The Wise Vet.
- Dog food and treats (for the "they actually take it" format): Pupford, Spot & Tango, Reggie.
- Searches: pills and picky dogs, senior dog and stairs, bad breath and kisses, shedding and fur, itchy skin, comment replies, taste tests. Human strip and patch brands came up empty for phone-filmable formats, so none made the list.

## What wins in this category
- The dog is the star and the phone is close. The top spenders (Dog is Human, PetLab, Wuffes) run almost entirely on owner footage: the dog on the couch, the scratch, the walk, the kiss.
- Dog-voice captions ("Mom, I wish you could tell...") are Dog is Human's top two ads by impressions. They work for both joints and itching.
- One strong prop or moment in the first second: the pill in the cheese, a jar of fur, a partner recoiling in bed, a red paw.
- Short skits with a twist (the morning breath that was the dog, a fake WARNING) get watched to the end.
- "Will my dog take it?" is the hidden doubt behind every supplement ad. Wuffes' pill-in-cheese ad and Pupford's picky dog ads go straight at it. That is VetriPaws' master angle, so it leads the board.

## What I rejected and why (the phone rule)
- PetLab "Did I just waste $40" (styled studio product shots), PetLab vet-in-scrubs breath ad (AI looking dog shots, graphics), PetLab tooth-in-powder demo (staged lab demo), PetLab 6s pour (just product).
- Nutri Paw joint ad (looks AI generated), Jope stairs ad (good green screen idea but turns into a 3D ingredient animation mid-way).
- Dinovite breath "let me guess" (CGI plaque animation inserts), Different Dog (studio set and brand cards).
- Native Pet dachshund breath (mostly bag shots), Zesty Paws Frenchie joint (mostly product b-roll), Zesty Paws cat POV (cat, and we have a dog POV), Groomi fur brush (a tool demo a strip can't copy), Reggie lick mat (weak link to a strip), second Dog is Human senior POV (same dog as the pick).
- Vet-lecture advertorials (Vet-Approved Pet Products, Tipaw) skipped: long doctor talks with b-roll.

## Checklist draft

**Before you film**
- Real dog, real home: your own dog, your own couch, phone held close. No studio, no slideshow.
- Show the strip go on: one unbroken shot of the strip on the tongue until it's gone.
- Hook in second one: start on the moment (the pill in the cheese, the fur, the kiss), not on hello.

**Say it right**
- "Supports" joints, breath, skin and coat. Never cures, treats or fixes.
- "Vet formulated" is fine. "Dissolves in about 30 seconds" is fine (that is the strip, not a result).
- "30 day money back guarantee" and "tin of 30 strips" are fine to mention.

**Skip these**
- Don't say "vet recommended", and no prices or discount codes on camera.
- Don't name or show another brand's product.
- No "results in 30 seconds" or before and after promises about health.
