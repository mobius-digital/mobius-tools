Launch Calendar

What's launching, when, and which channels need to care.

This is the shared picture of everything your brand is putting out over the next four weeks. It exists to answer three questions without anyone having to ask them in Slack:

What's coming up?

Everything launching in the next four weeks, and the work leading up to it.

Is that date real?

Locked, still soft, or wobbling — visible at a glance.

What do I have to do?

Filter to your channel and see only what involves you.

It is not a project manager. No tasks, no subtasks, no checklists. Your team keeps whatever workflow it already has. This sits above all of that as the one place everybody agrees on what is happening and when.

The three screens

Pipeline — your default view

This is the one to have open during a weekly marketing call. It shows four weeks, and it deliberately gets quieter as you look further out:

  • This week — full detail, plus a Mon–Sun strip showing the shape of the week at a glance.
  • Week 2 — one line per item.
  • Weeks 3 and 4 — a single summary line each. Click to open.

Launches and their run-up work are mixed together in date order — so "assets due Wednesday" sits above a launch that is still three weeks away, because that is the order things actually need doing.

Calendar — the timeline

The same information as a month or week grid. Each event stretches from the day teasers start through to the end of the promo, so you can see where things overlap and where the gaps are. Click any empty day to add something on that date.

Changelog — what changed

Every edit, recorded automatically. Nobody has to remember to write it down.

Reading the board

Is this date real?

StatusWhat it means
ConfirmedThe date is locked. Build against it.
TentativeStill soft and may move. Do not prep hard against it.
At riskWas confirmed, now in danger of slipping. Somebody should chase it.
CompletedShipped. Hidden from the board unless you tick "Show completed".
CancelledKilled. Hidden everywhere, but its history stays in the changelog.

Tentative events are drawn with a dashed, faded outline on purpose — so nobody accidentally builds a campaign around a date that is still a guess.

Run-up work

An event can have up to four dates besides its launch. The three that matter to other people show up on the board as their own entries:

🔔 Assets dueCreative and assets have to be finished
📣 Teasers startPre-launch comms begin
📦 Inventory landsProduct physically in hand

This is why one event can appear in two different weeks — its assets deadline in one, its launch in another.

Two warnings

clash  Two launches that both have a primary channel are landing within seven days of each other. Sometimes that is the plan — the flag just makes sure it is a decision, not an accident.

needs review  Nobody has touched this in three weeks and it launches within the month. Worth confirming it is still accurate. Opening and saving it clears the flag.

Channels — the most useful button

Along the top you will see All channels · Paid · Email · Organic · SMS. Pick yours, and the board narrows to only the events your channel is involved in, with your most important work emphasised.

Your choice is remembered, so you land pre-filtered every time. The web address changes too, so a filtered view can be pasted to a colleague.

What the priorities mean

PrimaryThis channel is building something. It is on the hook.
SupportingHelping, but not leading.
FYIJust needs to know it is happening.

Priorities are not decoration — the clash warning only fires on primary channels, because two big pushes colliding matters and two FYIs do not.

Adding and updating

Adding an event

  1. Click New event — or click a day on the Calendar to start with that date filled in.
  2. Fill in the name, a one-line brief, the launch date, which channels are involved, and who owns it.
  3. Add run-up dates if you know them. You can always come back.
  4. Save.

The first time you edit anything, it asks for your name. That name gets stamped on everything you touch, so the team can see who moved what.

Changing a status quickly

You do not need to open an event to change its status. Click the status button on any card and pick a new one. That is the main thing a weekly call produces — "that shipped", "that date is locked now", "that one is wobbling" — so it is one click.

Deleting

Set the status to Cancelled. It disappears from the planning views but its history survives. Permanent deletion is tucked behind a separate confirm in the editor, and you rarely want it.

Running your weekly meeting off it

  1. Open the Pipeline and share your screen. No prep needed.
  2. Read This week top to bottom. It is already in the order things need doing.
  3. Check Recent changes at the bottom — that is everything that moved since you last met.
  4. Deal with anything flagged clash or needs review.
  5. Update statuses live as you talk. Everyone else's board catches up within about ten seconds.
  6. Glance at Weeks 3 and 4 so nothing sneaks up.

Good habits

  • Be honest about Tentative. The whole system rests on people trusting that Confirmed means confirmed.
  • Every event needs an owner. That is who to ask, not who created it.
  • Write the brief for a media buyer. One sentence: what is the offer or angle.
  • Close things out. Mark launches Completed once they ship, or they pile up in the "past their launch date" strip.
  • Put run-up dates in. Assets and teaser dates are what make this useful to channels rather than just a list of launches.

Worth knowing

  • One shared password. No individual accounts. Anyone who is in can edit anything — the safeguard is that every card shows who last touched it and when.
  • Updates take about ten seconds. The board re-checks the server on a timer rather than instantly.
  • It works on a phone, though it is designed for a laptop or a shared screen.
  • Nothing is lost. Every date, status, channel and name change is recorded in the changelog with what it was before.
